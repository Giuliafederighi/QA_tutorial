01 · Your Mission

What You're Responsible For

Your core job is to ensure data quality by reviewing tasks completed by Taskers (annotators). You are validating that every conversation meets our standards before it's used for model training.

💬

Conversation Naturalness

The conversation must feel like a real person talking to an AI — fluid, coherent, and believable.

🔒

Hidden Context Integrity

Hidden context must stay hidden until naturally revealed — not leaked in the initial prompt or dumped early.

🤖

No AI Usage by Tasker

Taskers must write as humans. If AI-generated text is detected, the task is immediately rejected.

🎯

Persona & Scenario Fidelity

The Tasker must stay in character, respect the persona definition, and work within the scenario boundaries.

✅

Objective Evaluation

Pass/fail criteria must be graded with evidence, not vibes. Every decision must be traceable to specific turns.

📐

Complete Conversations

Conversations must reach a conclusive end. No abrupt stops, unfinished threads, or shallow interactions.

🧩 QA Mindset

Quality over speed. It's better to correct than to let errors pass. Always ask yourself: "Does this feel like a real conversation?" You are not just checking content — you are evaluating human behavior.

02 · Core Concept

Defining "Naturalness"

Naturalness is the central quality standard for every conversation. A natural conversation is one where the Tasker's messages are indistinguishable from what a real person in that situation would write. Here's how to evaluate it concretely.

✓ Evidence of Naturalness

  • Messages sound like a real person — casual, conversational, appropriate to the persona
  • Language matches what you'd expect (slang, abbreviations, level of formality for that persona)
  • Responses logically follow the AI's previous message
  • Hidden context emerges gradually, as a consequence of the conversation flow
  • The persona asks follow-up questions or reacts emotionally as a human would
  • The conversation progresses and explores the topic over time
  • Minor imperfections (typos, restarts, hesitation) can actually signal authenticity

✗ Evidence of Unnaturalness

  • Overly polished, formal, or "AI-like" phrasing from the Tasker
  • Excessive use of dashes (—), parentheses, semicolons, or complex punctuation
  • Repetitive sentence structures or expressions across turns
  • Messages that don't logically respond to what the AI just said
  • Dumping all hidden context in the first 1–2 turns
  • Persona speaking "out of character" (using expert terminology when they shouldn't)
  • Conversation feels scripted, robotic, or mechanical
  • Tasker seems to be guiding the AI to a specific answer rather than having a real conversation
💡 The Golden Question

Don't ask "Would I say this?" — ask "Would THIS persona say this, in THIS scenario?" You are judging the conversation as the persona, not as yourself.

03 · Review Process

QA Review Flowchart

Follow this process for every task you review. The phases are sequential — if a task fails at any phase, you take action before moving on.

Phase I — Scenario Details Check

Step 1 · Read All Scenario Details

Before looking at the conversation, read every element: User Persona, Scenario Context, Initial Prompt, Hidden Context, Expert Rationale, and Evaluation Criteria.

Decision · Are the elements realistic and coherent?

Check: Are User Persona, Scenario Context, and Initial Prompt consistent with each other? Does it feel like a real-world situation?

YES
Proceed to Phase II.
NO
STOP. Report to Vetto via the platform comment field. Do not continue.
Phase II — AI Usage Detection

Step 2 · Read the Full Conversation

Access the Conversation tab and read every turn between Tasker and AI. You must read the entire conversation before evaluating anything.

Decision · Does the Tasker appear to have used AI?

Look for: overly polished language, unnatural long dashes, templated phrasing, generic "AI-like" writing patterns, or suspiciously perfect structure.

NO AI detected
Proceed to Phase III.
AI detected
STOP. Reject the task immediately. Include a clear warning: risk of permanent ban from all Vector projects.
Phase III — Initial Prompt Evaluation

Step 3.1 · Does the Initial Prompt feel human?

Does it serve as a natural, spontaneous conversation starter? Would a real person open a conversation this way?

YES
Continue to 3.2.
NO
Request correction with a clear, specific comment explaining what needs to change.

Step 3.2 · Does the Initial Prompt leak Hidden Context?

If the Initial Prompt contains relevant Hidden Context information, did the conversation still develop naturally despite it? Revealing Hidden Context in turn 1–2 is a major red flag.

NO leak / naturalness preserved
Proceed to Phase IV.
Leaked / naturalness compromised
Request correction. Explain exactly which Hidden Context element was leaked and how it affects the conversation.
⚠️ #1 Source of Rework

Hidden context leaking in the initial prompt is the most common reason tasks are sent back for correction. Be especially vigilant here.

Phase IV — Conversation Quality

Step 4.1 · Persona Alignment

Are the Tasker's messages consistent with the User Persona definition? Does the Tasker stay in character throughout the entire conversation?

YES
Continue.
NO
Request correction. Specify where the Tasker broke character and how.

Step 4.2 · Language Quality

Is the language clear, accessible, and natural? Does the Tasker avoid excessive technical jargon, unusual punctuation (em dashes, parentheses), and repetitive patterns?

YES
Continue.
NO
Request correction with specific examples of problematic language.

Step 4.3 · Engagement & Coherence

Does the Tasker respond meaningfully to the AI's questions? Are responses coherent with previous turns? Does the Tasker engage actively — responding, asking follow-ups, and providing material for the AI to deepen the conversation?

YES
Continue.
NO
Request correction. Point out where responses were shallow, robotic, or incoherent.

Step 4.4 · Gradual Revelation of Hidden Context

Were Hidden Context elements revealed gradually and naturally? Did the Tasker reveal them only when the AI asked, or when it was a logical consequence of the conversation flow? If information was revealed early, did the conversation still feel natural?

YES
Continue.
NO
Request correction. Specify which Hidden Context elements were revealed too early or too directly.

Step 4.5 · Conversation Completion

Did the conversation reach a natural, conclusive end? Was the task's objective achieved? Did it avoid ending abruptly, prematurely, or inconclusively?

YES
Proceed to Phase V.
NO
Request correction. Explain what was left unresolved and what a complete conversation would include.
Phase V — Evaluation Criteria

Step 5 · Review Pass/Fail Criteria

Review the Tasker's pass/fail evaluations for each criterion. Check that every decision is supported by specific evidence from the conversation. Reasoning must be auditable — you should be able to trace each pass/fail back to what happened in which turn.

Decision · Are pass/fail evaluations correct and well-justified?

Do the evaluations match the conversation evidence? Is the reasoning clear, specific, and traceable?

All correct
✅ Approve the task.
Issues found
Request correction. Specify which criteria are wrong and why.
04 · Conduct Standards

Do's and Don'ts

These rules apply both when reviewing conversations and when evaluating. Keep them in mind at all times.

✓ DO
  • Read all scenario details before looking at the conversation
  • "Own" the task — understand what a good conversation looks like for this specific scenario
  • Evaluate naturalness from the persona's perspective, not your own
  • Reference specific turns when justifying pass/fail
  • Be strict and objective on evaluation criteria
  • Leave clear, actionable comments when sending back for correction
  • Ask in the Slack channel if anything is unclear
✗ DON'T
  • Don't use AI to complete the QA review
  • Don't score based on "vibes" — evidence only
  • Don't write vague justifications ("seemed fine", "it was good")
  • Don't let small inconsistencies slide — they compound
  • Don't proceed on autopilot if fields contradict each other
  • Don't evaluate the content/accuracy of the AI's responses — evaluate the Tasker's performance
  • Don't guess when unsure — ask the team
05 · Action Plan

Edge Cases & What To Do

Here's what to do when you encounter common problem scenarios during review.

Case 1 Hidden Context leaked in the Initial Prompt
This is the #1 source of rework. If the Initial Prompt contains information from the Hidden Context that compromises the natural development of the conversation, send it back. In your comment, specify: which element was leaked, where exactly (quote the prompt section), and how it affects the conversation's ability to develop naturally. If the Hidden Context is present but the conversation still developed naturally, use your judgment — but err on the side of flagging it.
Case 2 AI usage detected in Tasker's messages
Reject immediately. Do not attempt to correct — this is a zero-tolerance policy. In your rejection comment, include a clear warning that the annotator risks permanent ban from all Vector projects. Signs of AI usage: overly polished language, unnatural punctuation patterns, templated phrasing, generic "AI-like" structure, or suspiciously consistent formatting across all turns.
Case 3 Robotic or nonsensical conversation
Request correction with clear guidance. Point out specific examples of what feels unnatural: repetitive messages, responses that don't connect to the AI's previous turn, language that doesn't match the persona, or patterns that feel scripted. Explain what a natural version would look like for this particular scenario and persona.
Case 4 Incomplete or interrupted conversation
Request continuation or improvement. In your comment, indicate that the task objective was not achieved. Specify what's missing: was the conversation cut short? Was the context insufficiently developed? Were key elements of the Hidden Context never explored? The conversation must reach a natural, conclusive end with evidence that the scenario's goal was met.
Case 5 Scenario details are unrealistic or contradictory
STOP. Do not proceed with the review. Report the issue to the Vetto team via the platform comment field. Include: what looks odd (which field/criterion), why it looks odd (what inconsistency you found), and how it may affect the conversation or evaluation. Wait for team guidance before continuing.
Case 6 Vague or generic pass/fail justifications
Request correction. The Tasker's reasoning must be auditable: anyone reading it should understand what happened, where it happened, and why it supports the pass/fail decision. Examples of unacceptable justification: "AI was effective", "seemed fine", "persona mismatch" without proof. Explain what level of specificity is required — e.g., "Passed because in turn 3 the AI asked about X, which directly addresses criterion Y."
06 · Common Pitfalls

Common Mistakes & What Good Looks Like

These are the most frequent errors new QAers make. Learn from them before your first review.

Judging as yourself

Evaluating based on what you would say instead of what the persona would say.

✗ "I wouldn't phrase it like that"
✓ "Would this 45-year-old teacher with limited tech knowledge phrase it this way?"

Vague justifications

Writing generic pass/fail reasoning that can't be verified.

✗ "The conversation was natural and the AI helped well."
✓ "Passed — in turn 4 the AI probed income volatility, and in turn 6 it recommended a buffer fund, directly addressing the criterion."

Skipping context first

Jumping straight to the conversation without reading scenario details.

✗ Reading the chat first, then checking persona
✓ Reading Persona → Scenario → Hidden Context → then the conversation

Ignoring the "knife"

Missing when the key piece of information (the knife) is dropped too early or too directly.

✗ Not noticing hidden context was fully revealed in turn 1
✓ Flagging: "The knife (income instability) was revealed explicitly in the initial prompt, compromising the conversation's natural development."

10 · Writing Quality

How to Write Strong Reviews

Your review comments need to be clear and auditable. If someone asks "why did you pass/fail this?", you should be able to explain your logic in detail.

Structure for every justification

What happened — describe the specific event or behavior

Where it happened — reference the specific turn or section of the conversation

Why it matters — connect it to the scenario, persona, or evaluation criterion

Correction comment

✗ "The prompt has issues, please fix."
✓ "The initial prompt mentions the user's $50k debt directly, which is part of the Hidden Context (financial stress). This removes the opportunity for the AI to discover it naturally. Rewrite the opening without referencing specific debt amounts."

Pass/fail justification

✗ "The AI gave good advice."
✓ "Passed: In turn 5, the AI asked about income stability. In turn 7, it probed monthly expenses. By turn 9, it synthesized both into a tailored savings plan — this directly satisfies the 'financial assessment probe' criterion."
